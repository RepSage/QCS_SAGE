# QCS v9.1 — the light series tells you when the logger's clock is wrong

> **MINOR** release (v9.0 → v9.1). The new check emits **warnings only** — it
> adds no flag character and changes no threshold, so re-qualifying any file
> with the shipped settings produces the identical flags. What *does* change is
> three products of the archive, whose raw exports carry a clock error that is
> now corrected explicitly (below).

## Where this came from

A user noticed that a 2022S1 HOBO had ~60000 lux in its first week while the
rest of the series never came close, and concluded the fouling cutoff had fired
too early. Chasing that produced a different answer entirely.

The first week was not an outlier: the daily thermal amplitude over it was
1.1–1.6 °C, which is water, not air (a logger on deck swings 8–15 °C). The
deployment also ran August → March, winter into summer, so ambient light should
have *risen*. Nothing about the decline was natural.

The raw exports said why:

```
PLES:  08/21/21 09:21:38 PM -> 13089 lux      (13 thousand lux at 21:21)
SGOM:  08/20/21 11:08:39 PM -> 31689 lux      (31 thousand lux at 23:08)
TIM2:  08/25/21 05:00:00 AM ->   312 lux  /  09:00 AM -> 0 lux
```

**Three loggers had been launched with AM/PM swapped.** Their whole series —
light and temperature — sits 12 h out of phase.

**It did not, however, explain the cutoff that started the hunt.** Correcting
the clock and re-qualifying left the fouling verdicts essentially unchanged
(PLES: 5 of 209 days reach the threshold before and after; SGOM: 9 of 213,
last one on the same date). The daily peak is a per-calendar-day maximum, and a
uniform 12 h shift regroups the samples without moving that maximum much, so
the statistic happened to be robust to this particular error. The clock defect
is real and corrupts every time-of-day result and both channels' timestamps —
but the cutoff behaviour it was found while chasing remains an open question,
tracked separately.

## The check: `light_clock_phase` (`QCS_Tests.py`)

A submerged light sensor must peak near local noon. The test measures the
**circular** mean hour-of-day weighted by light (24 h is a circle: a plain mean
would put a peak spanning midnight at noon) and the share of light energy
recorded between 06:00 and 18:00 — the blunt, decisive number, since a sound
logger scores ~100% and a 12 h-shifted one ~0%.

It runs **before** the fouling analysis in the HOBO pipeline, because a wrong
clock makes every daily light statistic below it meaningless, and the operator
must see that before being asked to approve a cutoff. It is a property of the
whole series, so it emits a warning and a measurement, not a flag character —
the `FLAG_BUCKET_MAP` layout is untouched.

When it accuses, the prescribed shift is **exactly 12 h**, never the measured
11.2/11.5/11.6: an AM/PM mistake is exact antiphase, and the centroid lands off
noon only because cloud and fouling are not symmetric about it. Rounding the
centroid would leave the series an hour wrong.

Validated against the whole qualified archive: it accuses **the three known-bad
loggers and nobody else**, prescribing +12 h for all three.

### It also names a second, different 12-hour failure

Some HOBOware exports (pt-BR locale, times written `04h0min0s`) carry a 12-hour
clock **with no AM/PM marker at all**, so every afternoon reading lands on top of
its morning twin:

```
1,03/17/18 04h0min0s,21.473      <- 04:00
3,03/17/18 10h0min0s,28.953      <- 10:00
4,03/17/18 01h0min0s,28.555      <- 13:00, written as 01h
```

Its signature is unmistakable — no sample after 12:59 and about half the
timestamps duplicated. The check reports this as `collapsed`, deliberately
*without* prescribing a shift: the remedy is to reconstruct the afternoon half
from the row order, not to move the series. **53 of the 116 qualified HOBO
products carry it**; that repair is not in this release.

## The three corrected products

`CLOCK_CORRECTIONS` in `sourceCode/batch/qualify_site.py` lists the eight raw
exports behind PLES, SGOM and TIM2 2022S1 with a −12 h shift and the evidence for
each. It is applied by patching the handler's `read_hobo`, so every path —
qualification, span probing, replicate grouping — sees one corrected time axis;
a correction applied in one place and not the others would silently split the
corpus into two timebases.

After correction the three land exactly on the phase of the sound loggers of the
same semester:

| product | before | after | reference (sound, same semester) |
|---|---|---|---|
| PLES 2022S1 | 23.2 h, 0.8% daylight | 11.2 h, 99.2% | PAB3 11.6 h, 99.9% |
| SGOM 2022S1 | 23.5 h, 0.0% | 11.5 h, 100% | PNOR 11.8 h, 99.9% |
| TIM2 2022S1 | 23.6 h, 0.0% | 11.6 h, 100% | ESQRODO 11.7 h, 100% |

## Verification

42 self-tests pass (`QCS_SelfTest.py`), ruff clean. Test #24 covers a sound
logger, a 12 h swap named and reversed, the collapsed 12-hour clock refusing to
prescribe a shift, and a heavily fouled but correctly-clocked logger **not**
being accused — fouling attenuates the light, it does not move the peak.
