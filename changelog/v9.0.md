# QCS v9.0 — the replicate referee: naming WHICH redundant HOBO is the sound one

> **MAJOR** release (v8.1 → v9.0). It changes qualified output: when two HOBO
> replicates disagree and the evidence is conclusive, the run can now qualify
> from ONE of them instead of averaging both. Re-qualifying an affected
> deployment with v9.0 produces different values and different flags than v8.0.

## The problem this release solves

Redundant HOBO loggers are deployed in pairs. Until now the pipeline averaged
the pair and ran a spread test that flagged disagreement as SUSPECT. That is
honest but useless in the one case that matters: **when one of the two sensors
is broken, the average is contaminated and the flag does not repair it.**
Dropping the SUSPECT rows is no fix either, because they carry the sound
replicate as well.

The case that exposed it: **PLES 2022S2**. From ~1 May 2022 replicate 1 flatlines
around 28.5–29.8 °C — it even *rises* to 29.79 °C in September, mid winter — while
its twin cools 28.5 → 24.4 °C. The combined product carried a huge error bar for
half the series, and the seasonal signal was destroyed. Every single-series test
passed replicate 1 as GOOD: the values are perfectly plausible temperatures, they
are just the wrong ones. **No test that looks at one series in isolation can catch
a sensor stuck on a plausible value.**

## The referee (`replicate_referee` in `QCS_Tests.py`)

The insight is that the decision cannot come from the pair itself — with two
disagreeing series and nothing else, there is no way to tell which one lies. So
the referee brings in a **third, independent witness**: the contemporaneous
loggers at *other sites*. Regional temperature moves together; a sound logger
follows it, a broken one does not.

It runs in two steps.

**Step 1 — is there a disagreement at all?** The replicates must differ by more
than `temp_tol` (0.5 °C) over at least `min_frac` (20 %) of the overlap and at
least `min_points` samples. Below that the pair agrees and nothing happens: the
run averages them exactly as before.

**Step 2 — which one is right?** Each replicate is scored against the reference
series on three independent criteria, and a verdict is issued only if the winner
beats the loser by `min_margin`:

1. **Change-correlation** — correlation of *day-to-day changes* (not absolute
   values, which a constant offset would dominate) with the reference. This is
   the primary criterion, and it needs `min_ref_corr` (0.50) to count at all.
2. **Offset drift** — a replicate whose bias against the reference *shifts* over
   the deployment is drifting; a genuine depth/site offset stays put.
3. **Seasonal amplitude** — the ratio of the replicate's swing to the
   reference's. Far too damped (`amp_bad`, 2×) or far too exaggerated is wrong.

Criteria 2 and 3 measure a replicate *against the reference*, so they only mean
anything when the reference genuinely describes this site. They are gated behind
`min_ref_corr_secondary` (0.70): if the reference tracks the site weakly, the
referee **abstains** rather than arbitrating with a bad yardstick.

**What the referee deliberately does NOT do:** it does not apply a fixed seasonal
rule ("warm in summer, cool in winter"). Encoding the expected answer and then
selecting the data that matches it is confirmation bias, and it would silently
destroy any genuine anomaly — a marine heatwave, an upwelling event. The
reference is measured, contemporaneous, and independent; the expectation is
never hardcoded.

Every threshold lives in `REPLICATE_REFEREE_DEFAULTS`, and the referee returns
its full score table, so a verdict is always auditable.

## The review window (Qualification tab)

When the referee names a replicate, the run **stops and shows the evidence**
before writing anything: both replicates plotted against the reference, the
score table, and the verdict in words. The operator chooses:

- **Accept** — qualify from the named replicate alone. The dropped replicate is
  recorded in the product's provenance with the full reason.
- **Keep all** — fall back to the v8.0 behaviour (average both, spread test).

The decision is never taken silently: with no reference available, or with the
replicates too close to separate, the referee reports that it *cannot* decide and
the run proceeds by averaging, unchanged.

Excluding the PAB3 2021S1 replicate **recovered a product that had been lost

timestamps"), which aborted the whole deployment. `PAB3_2021S1_HOBO_3_QLF` now
qualifies with 5611 rows.

## Fixes

- **Duplicated timestamps.** Real exports carry them (one file has 8833), and
  `reindex` refuses to work on a duplicated axis. The referee now de-duplicates
  each series before aligning. Found by running on real data, not by the three
  clean synthetic tests.
- **Provenance named the wrong products.** An excluded replicate was stamped on
  every product of its campaign folder, including deployments it was never part
  of. Exclusions are now tied to the deployment they belonged to, by span.
