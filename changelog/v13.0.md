# QCS v13.0 - the current profiler gets its manual review

> **MAJOR** release (v12.3 -> v13.0). Qualified CURRENT values change: the
> current flag string carries a fifth position (the operator's manual review),
> and a manual dismissal writes flag 5 over the whole row and blanks its
> measurements. Scalar (Seaguard TSCP) and HOBO qualification are untouched -
> no flag, threshold or test logic changed for them.

## Data qualification

- **A DCPS session can now be reviewed by hand.** The current pipeline
  returned before any interactive stop, so a current-profiler session was the
  only product nobody could correct: everything the automatic tests missed
  stayed in the sheet. It now stops twice, between reading the session and
  running the tests.
- **The tilt review always runs, one point per record.** Tilt is the DCPS's
  equivalent of the Depth review: it is a record-level series, so cutting a
  point dismisses the whole record - every depth cell of that instant - which
  is how deployment and recovery leave a mooring record. The cut cannot be
  made on depth: in a current table `Depth (m)` is the CELL depth, a handful
  of fixed values repeated on every record.
- **"Check variables" offers the variables a current table actually has**:
  horizontal speed, direction, vertical speed, speed stdev and signal
  strength. It used to offer the nine scalar variables, none of which exists
  in a current sheet, so the chooser came up empty. Each series is shown one
  depth cell at a time, in sequence, instead of the comb the record-major row
  order draws; the tilt cuts carry over locked, so they are not offered twice.
- **A manual dismissal condemns the whole cell sample.** Speed, direction and
  the U/V/W components of a cell are one velocity solution per ping ensemble,
  with the stdev and the signal strength as its own diagnostics: a point the
  operator cannot trust is not one bad column. Every flag position of the row
  becomes 5 and every measurement is blanked, while the row keeps its
  Datetime, Site, Column, Cell and Depth so the cut stays traceable. A cut
  RECORD also loses its attitude context; a cut CELL keeps it, because the
  record is still sound for its other cells.
- **The current flag string has five positions.** The fifth is the manual
  review: 2 when no review ran, 1 when it ran and kept the row, 5 when the
  operator dismissed it. The flag legend written beside the sheet lists it and
  explains the dismissal rule. A current table qualified by an earlier version
  carries four characters and must be requalified to gain the position.
- **The tilt review says what the instrument was doing.** The panel draws the
  two QC thresholds (suspect and bad, as currently set) and shades every span
  where the tilt is 45 degrees or more - lying over, being handled, hauled up
  or on deck - the way the Depth review shades the transits of a scalar
  mooring. On the reference session that band covers the deployment and the
  whole last third of the record, where the instrument went over at 80 degrees
  and stayed there. 45 degrees is the midpoint of a plateau: 299 records
  exceed 35 degrees, 299 exceed 45 and 298 exceed 60, so any threshold in that
  range selects the same events, and past 45 the instrument is closer to
  horizontal than to upright.
- **"Remove dismissed data" applies to a current sheet too**: every dismissal
  there is a whole-row one, so the dismissed rows leave the output when the
  option is ticked, exactly as the Depth review's cuts do for a scalar file.

## Data qualification (both instruments)

- **A Seaguard session says whether it is a mooring or a cast**, and the Data
  type is set from it when the file is chosen: a cast is lowered and recovered
  in minutes and logged fast, a mooring is left down for hours to weeks and
  logged slowly. Measured over the whole archive - 182 labelled scalar
  sessions, 2019 to 2026 - "four hours long OR five minutes between records"
  names 181 of them right; its only miss is a 16-record session that logs every
  10 minutes inside a cast folder. It is a SUGGESTION and never a lock: the
  type decides which tests run, so the box stays editable, the log says what was
  detected and why, and the Selection summary carries the session's length. A
  session too short to time says so instead of guessing.
- **The pipeline checks the Data type against the session it just read**, in
  every shell and in the batch drivers, and warns when the two disagree -
  naming the duration, the cadence and what the tests will do about it. The
  choice is honoured either way.
- **The buttons of the manual point-cut panels look like the program's**, with
  a border and the platform's own colours instead of matplotlib's flat grey
  boxes.

## Plots

- **The time axis of the current panels is readable.** The labels are rotated
  45 degrees, anchored under their own tick, and the axis picks a tick count it
  can fit; the date and the year move to the offset line at the right end
  instead of being repeated 15 times. On a three-day session the old fixed
  format printed a solid line of overprinted text.
- **The direction colour key is a compass wheel.** A linear 0-360 bar puts
  north at both ends and never says which colour is east; on the wheel the
  colour IS the bearing, labelled N/E/S/W. Its label is lettered like the speed
  panel's own colour bar - same font, same size, read from the figure rather
  than hand-picked - and clears the N mark by a margin that follows the font.
  The heatmap it describes is unchanged.

## Data visualization

- **The current panels open in one window, paged.** *< Previous* and *Next >*
  walk through them at the operator's pace, each page keeping its own
  navigation toolbar, and the title says which panel of how many. They used to
  open as one window per figure - four per site, plus the cross-site
  comparison. The scalar and HOBO panels still open side by side, which is how
  they are meant to be compared.
- **A Doppler database no longer shows settings it cannot use.** The three
  panel checkboxes (still carrying the Seaguard labels), the parameter filter,
  the whole Scale settings column, "Show data points", "Tendency lines" and
  the regression degree are gone for this instrument: the four current panels
  have fixed content and are heatmaps, sticks and a trajectory - there is no
  series to mark or fit. Sites, years, the time window and the depth band all
  still apply.
- **The post-run shortcuts go away when the selection changes.** "Open output
  folder" and "Go to visualization" point at the run that just finished, so
  they disappear the moment another file is chosen or the field is cleared -
  they used to stay, pointing at the previous run's folder.
- **"Fixed scale" says which scale it means.** On a current database it reads
  *Fixed scale (one speed colour scale)* and explains that the scale is
  computed from the data - nothing is typed. On the scalar families it stays
  the Min/Max the operator types per parameter, and the tooltip now says so.

## Note

- **What is blank in a current panel is what the instrument refused to
  measure.** On the reference session 8,636 of 13,408 cell samples are BAD, and
  every one of them was condemned by the DCPS's own cell-state word - of the
  4,772 cells the instrument calls good, QCS rejects none. The pattern is
  geometry: the three cells nearest the transducer (1, 2.5 and 3 m) and the
  farthest one (29 m) are 100% rejected, the middle of the water column between
  40 and 60%, and the last day of the deployment - the instrument lying at 80
  degrees - is rejected whole.

## Verification

- Self-test suite 57/57, ruff clean. The whole current pipeline was run
  end to end on a real DCPS session (13,408 cell samples, 838 records x 16
  cells) three times: with no cut, with a tilt cut plus per-cell cuts, and
  with the same cuts under "Remove dismissed data". Without a cut it
  reproduces v12.3 exactly - 4,646 good, 126 suspect, 8,636 bad; with the
  cuts, 95 rows became flag 5 (`55555`, all measurements blank) and left the
  sheet when the option was on. The visualization changes were measured by
  driving the real Qt shell off-screen over a qualified current table and a
  qualified scalar table, checking that the scalar page keeps its three panel
  boxes, its twelve parameters and its scale column. The mooring/cast detection
  was measured on 182 labelled archive sessions and then driven through the
  real shell on four of them (a 97 h mooring, a 3 h mooring, a 50 min cast and
  a one-record session), and through the pipeline on a real mooring qualified
  both correctly and, on purpose, as a profile.
