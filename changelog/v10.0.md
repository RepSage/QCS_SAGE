# QCS v10.0 — the adaptive light rule reads season, not just lux

> **MAJOR** release (v9.1 → v10.0). It changes QC results: the adaptive light
> cutoff now compares each daily peak against the **astronomical clear-sky
> curve** for the run's latitude, so re-qualifying a HOBO file in adaptive mode
> can produce different `Flag_lux` than v9.1. The **corpus is unaffected** — it
> is qualified in fixed-60d mode, and a corpus product requalified under v10.0
> is byte-identical (verified). Ships together with the unreleased v9.1 (see
> `changelog/v9.1.md`).

## The problem

The adaptive rule anchors its threshold to the first week and then reads the
decline out of the light itself. But ambient light has a deterministic seasonal
component: at the Abrolhos latitude (18 °S) the clear-sky ceiling falls to
**~0.69 of its summer value** at the winter solstice. A deployment installed in
February–April walks into winter — its daily peaks fall with no fouling at all —
and one installed June–September walks into summer, where rising ambient light
masks a genuinely fouling sensor. Measured on the corpus (111 light series,
shipped rule): deployments walking into falling light were **over-cut (>90% of
the light discarded) in 37% of cases vs 12%** walking into rising light.

## The fix: normalize by the clear-sky curve

`clear_sky_factor(dates, latitude)` in `QCS_Tests.py`: the sine of the noon
solar elevation with standard atmospheric attenuation (transmittance 0.75),
normalized to the annual maximum at that latitude. `light_fouling_baseline`
gained a `latitude` parameter: when given, every daily peak is divided by the
factor for its day of year, so the baseline, the threshold and every crossing
run on **"fraction of the seasonal ceiling"** instead of raw lux. A clean
sensor is a flat normalized series regardless of install month; a fouling one
declines in any season.

The latitude comes from the **region selection** in the Qualification tab
(always resolvable — the combobox has a default), which is therefore now
enabled for HOBO too. In the saved `QCS_light_window.svg` the baseline and
threshold become **curves** that breathe with the season — a flat line would
misrepresent the rule actually applied — and the rule text names the latitude.
`latitude=None` reproduces the v9.1 rule exactly.

## The curve was chosen against the corpus, not assumed

Three deterministic candidates were simulated over the 111 light series with
the shipped adaptive parameters:

| curve | over-cut falling / rising | asymmetry | annual amplitude at 18 °S |
|---|---|---|---|
| none (v9.1) | 37% / 12% | 25 p.p. | — |
| noon elevation | 37% / 15% | 22 p.p. | 0.75 |
| **noon + airmass (T=0.75)** | **34% / 15%** | **19 p.p.** | 0.69 |
| daily-integrated H0 | 34% / 15% | 19 p.p. | 0.60 |

Noon-with-airmass wins on physics (the daily *peak* is a noon quantity; H0's
day-length term affects the daily total, not the peak) and ties on numbers.
The transmittance is the textbook 0.75 and deliberately **not tuned**: the
result is flat from T=0.75 to 0.55, and fitting the correction to the archive
it will then judge would be circular.

## Honest limits — why the fixed window remains the corpus standard

The correction removes the **deterministic** part of the seasonal confound and
no more: over-cutting on falling-light deployments goes 37% → 34%, spurious
keeps of dark sensors 20% → 15%, and the falling-vs-rising asymmetry 25 → 19
percentage points. The residual seasonal signal — winter cloud and turbidity —
dominates the deep declines and is not predictable from astronomy. That is why
the corpus stays on the fixed 60-day window (v9.1): the adaptive mode is now
*better*, not *solved*.

## Also hardened (adversarial review before shipping, 6 confirmed findings)

- **The batch driver now pins the region** before every run: the GUI restores
  the last hand-selected region from the gitignored user prefs, so a corpus
  re-run on a machine whose last GUI session used another region would have
  computed the seasonal curves at the wrong latitude — silently, since the
  fixed-mode flags do not change. The corpus is Abrolhos; batch must not
  depend on GUI leftovers.
- The review window's **Help dialog** now describes the season-corrected rule
  (it still described the flat raw-lux rule while the plot in front of the
  operator showed curves).
- Self-test #26 was **hardened by mutation testing**: two mutants survived the
  original assertions (baseline taken from raw peaks while crossings run
  corrected; threshold curve inverted to `threshold/factor`, drawing the winter
  threshold ~2× too high on every review plot). Both are now killed by value
  assertions.
- Three stale comments still claiming the region is disabled for HOBO were
  updated — the sole input path of the latitude must not carry instructions to
  disable itself.

## Verification

44 self-tests pass (`QCS_SelfTest.py`), ruff clean. Test #26 covers: the factor
(~1 at the summer ceiling, ~0.68 at the austral winter solstice, hemispheres
mirror); a season-shaped decline that fires the uncorrected rule and must NOT
fire the corrected one; genuine exponential fouling still caught at the decay's
own crossing (~day 28), not the season's; and `latitude=None` byte-compatible
with the old rule. A fixed-mode corpus product requalified under v10.0 is
byte-identical to its v9.1 output.
