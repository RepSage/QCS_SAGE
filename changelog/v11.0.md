# QCS v11.0 — temperatures that lost their decimal point, and loggers with no light sensor

> **MAJOR** release (v10.0 → v11.0). Both changes are in the reader, so they
> alter what qualification produces. Unlike the aborted first attempt at a
> "v11.0", this one really does change `sourceCode/*.py` outside `batch/`.

## Five corpus products carried temperatures in the tens of thousands

Some HOBOware xlsx exports wrote **25.125 °C as the integer 25125** — the pt-BR
comma decimal was dropped when the workbook was written, and the reader took the
number at face value:

```
04/05/24 07h6min49s | 25125 | 21.5      <- 25.125 degC, not 25125
```

Found while chasing something else entirely: a logger whose light and
temperature "disagreed" about a clock offset. They did not disagree — the
temperature was a thousand times too large, so its diurnal phase was noise.
A sweep then showed **five products out of the whole corpus** in the tens of
thousands of degrees: ESQNORTE 2026S1, PAB3 2024S2 (both), RODORASO 2023S2 and
ESQRODO 2025S1.

`_hobo_fix_temp_scale` recovers it, and is deliberately hard to trigger:

- essentially every value must be outside −5…60 °C (a merely warm file is left
  alone), **and**
- the values must be **integers** — a lost separator leaves integers behind,
  while a sensor genuinely reading −84.77 °C still carries its decimals, **and**
- one single power of ten must bring essentially all of them back.

That middle rule matters more than it looks. Without it, a broken sensor
reading −84.77…156.53 °C would be "recovered" by dividing by 100 into
−0.85…1.57 °C — absurd for a tropical reef, but inside any tolerance wide
enough to be useful. The self-test carries exactly that case: it was a real
false positive in the first draft of the rule.

Anything out of range that is *not* rescalable is reported and left untouched.
Inventing plausible-looking data is worse than an obviously absurd number.

## Loggers that record temperature only

Some Pendant models have no light sensor. The reader already tolerated them —
their `Luminosity` column is simply empty — but two things downstream still
treated them as broken:

- the collapsed-clock repair refused them for "too little light to tell morning
  from afternoon". It now sends them to the **temperature** fallback added in
  the previous round: the half whose daily temperature peak falls nearer the
  corpus median (13.7 h) wins. `PAB3_30062016_PAREDE.csv` — 17,668 rows with
  8,833 duplicated timestamps, the file that first exposed the referee's dedup
  problem back in v9.0 — is repaired by that route;
- corpus products are now named `..._TEMP_ONLY_QLF`, so an empty light column
  reads as "this logger has no light sensor" and never as "the light data went
  missing".
